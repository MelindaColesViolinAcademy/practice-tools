Down bow and up bow

The two words that confuse almost every beginner, the symbols you will see above the notes, and how to work out your own bowing when the music does not tell you.

The thing to understand first

Down bow does not mean the bow points down. It is easy to get confused by this. When you play a down bow on the G string it will actually look as though you are pulling the bow up into the sky — and an up bow on the G or D string will look as though the bow is pointing down towards the ground.

Down and up describe which end of the bow you start from, not which way it points in the room.

The two symbols

These appear above the note when the music tells you which bow direction to use.

Down bow Begin at the frog and pull towards the tip.
Up bow Begin at the tip and push towards the frog.

Marking your own bowing

Not all music comes marked. It is often worth pencilling in your own, and as a rule you want to start a bar on a down bow.

  • Down bows on the strong beats — one and three. Up bows on two and four.
  • A new group of quavers or semiquavers should begin on a down bow.
  • Pick-up notes — the note leading into the start of a piece or bar — take an up bow, so the next bar can begin on a down.

Mark a bar yourself

Four beats. Tap each one to set a down or up bow, then check it against the rule.

Arm weight

There should be a relaxed, weighty feeling in the arm. Work through these five with your bow arm and notice what happens.

  1. Let your right arm hang beside you
  2. Form an L
  3. Raise the elbow
  4. Notice the effort
  5. Let it fall
