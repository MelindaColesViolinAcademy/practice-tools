Melinda Coles Violin Academy · Assignment

Legato, rhythm and first position

Six sections, thirty to forty minutes in total. Each one has its own timer. Nothing here needs doing in a single sitting — spread it over a few days if that suits you better.

What this assignment is for

Building consistency in tone, rhythm and the coordination between your two hands, while staying relaxed. Four focus areas: legato bowing, pizzicato control, rhythm accuracy, and left-hand finger placement in first position.

1. Warm-up

5 to 8 minutes
  • Open strings: G, D, A, E
  • Four slow bows on each string
Listen for

An even sound from frog to tip · no accent at the bow change · a relaxed shoulder and wrist

2. Legato

8 to 10 minutes

Exercise A — two-note slurs

  • Stay on one string. On the A string, use B to C♯
  • Two notes to a bow
  • Repeat slowly, many times

Exercise B — four-note slurs

  • Use the scale pattern A – B – C♯ – D
  • One bow for the whole group
Listen for

A smooth connection with no gaps · consistent bow speed · quiet direction changes

3. Pizzicato

5 minutes
  • Pluck the open strings
  • Then simple finger patterns — A, B, C♯
Listen for

A clean release without pulling the string too far · even rhythm · a relaxed hand after every pluck

4. Rhythm

5 to 8 minutes

A. Busy Busy Stop Stop

Busy Busy is four half notes. Stop Stop is two whole notes. Sixteen beats altogether.

Count aloud: 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 | 7–8 | hold | hold

B. Quavers

Clap or play, counting out loud: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &

Listen for

Even spacing · and above all, not rushing the "and"

Open the rhythm trainer

5. Finger placement

5 to 8 minutes
  • On the D and A strings
  • The pattern 0 – 1 – 2 – 3 – 4, up and back down
  • Remember the second finger is high — up against the third
Listen for

Accurate spacing · curved fingers · as little tension as you can manage

Open the fingering trainer

6. Put it together

5 to 10 minutes

Play a simple four to eight bar piece, or combine the pieces yourself:

  • Legato — slurred notes
  • Quavers
  • Simple fingering in first position

Finish on something that sounds like music, however short.

Four things to include.

By the end of this you should be able to

  • Produce a steady legato tone
  • Understand basic rhythm subdivisions
  • Control pizzicato cleanly
  • Place your fingers more accurately in first position
Thirty to forty minutes altogether. If you only have fifteen, do sections one, four and five — those three carry the most.
