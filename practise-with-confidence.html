A note from Melinda

You do not have to rush any of this, and you do not have to do it well. Showing up is the part that compounds. The playing follows.

The five stages

1Get ready Set up your violin, bow and music calmly, before you play a note. Rushing this makes everything after it harder.
2Begin slowly Take a breath and start. Slowly is not a compromise — it is the correct speed for learning.
3Play clearly Work through carefully: open strings, posture, bow hold, then whatever you are learning.
4Listen Notice which sound felt easier, steadier, more in tune. Noticing is a skill of its own and it comes before fixing.
5Adjust gently Make one small change. If something feels unsure or uncomfortable, stop and ask rather than pushing through.

A few questions, before you put it away

  • What felt easier today than last time?
  • Which string sounded best?
  • What would you like to repeat next time?
  • Did you stay calm while you practised?

You do not have to write the answers down. Just asking them turns practice into learning.
