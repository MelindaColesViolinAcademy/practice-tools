Bow studio

A quiet place to build a relaxed bow hold, a straighter bow, and smoother open strings. Everything here is meant to be done slowly.

A note from Melinda

The bow is where most of the sound lives, and it takes longer to learn than the left hand. Be patient with it. Slow, relaxed and a bit boring is exactly right.

Setting the hand

Four steps. Build it, take it apart, build it again — that is the exercise, not a chore before the exercise.

  1. Thumb soft and bent
  2. Fingers curved, not gripping
  3. Little finger rounded on top
  4. Hand balanced, never stiff

Straight bow trainer

Drag the green bow left and right, following the gold dot, and keep it on the dashed line. A real long bow takes six to ten seconds — set it slower than feels comfortable. Drifting up toward the bridge or down toward the fingerboard is exactly what a crooked bow does on a real violin.

Slow bow work

Three exercises. Twenty seconds each is plenty — it is longer than it sounds.

  • Silent bow hold. Build the hand, feel where the tension is, release it, take it apart. Repeat.
  • Air bows. Move the bow slowly through the air with no violin. Watch that it travels straight.
  • Open-string long bows. One whole bow per twenty seconds, listening for the sound staying even.
