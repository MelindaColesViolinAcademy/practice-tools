Remembering the parts

Ten parts, from the scroll at the top to the shoulder rest underneath. Learn them once and you will have them for good.

A note from Melinda

Work from top to bottom, and say the names out loud while pointing at each part on your own violin. Saying them aloud does far more than reading them — it is how the names actually stick.

The ten parts

Top to bottom, in order.

  • ScrollAt the very top, carved and curled. It balances the instrument and is the maker's signature.
  • PegsFour of them, in the pegbox. These are what you tune with.
  • NutHolds the strings in place at the top of the fingerboard, and sets their height.
  • FingerboardThe long black strip your left-hand fingers press the strings against.
  • StringsFour of them, running from the pegs all the way down to the tailpiece.
  • BridgeHolds the strings up and carries their vibration into the body. Not glued — held by string tension alone.
  • F-holesThe two shaped openings that let the sound out of the body.
  • BodyThe hollow wooden box that makes the sound. Everything else is in service of this.
  • Chin restSits on top, left of the tailpiece. Your jaw settles here to hold the violin.
  • Shoulder restClips on underneath, resting on your shoulder. It fills the gap so you do not have to.

Tap a hidden name to reveal it.

A rhyme to remember them by

At the top is the scroll, curled up just right,
Turn the pegs to tune — not too tight!

Past the nut to the fingerboard long,
Where fingers press to make notes strong.

The strings sing over the bridge so tall,
Past the f-holes where the music calls.

The body makes the sound ring true,
Chin rest and shoulder rest help hold it for you!

Say it once out loud while pointing at each part. Then say it again with your eyes closed. That is usually all it takes.
